Protein function is determined by its three-dimensional structure. Structural bioinformatics uses computational methods to predict, analyse, and visualise protein structures — revolutionised by AlphaFold, which can predict structures with near-experimental accuracy.

Proteins fold into defined 3D structures that determine their function. Understanding the four levels of protein structure is fundamental to structural bioinformatics and drug design.

The Four Levels of Structure

Primary (1°)

The linear amino acid sequence. Determines all higher levels. Read from gene sequence. 20 standard amino acids linked by peptide bonds.

Secondary (2°)

Local folding patterns: α-helices (3.6 residues per turn, stabilised by i→i+4 hydrogen bonds) and β-sheets (parallel or antiparallel strands). Loops connect regular elements.

Tertiary (3°)

The complete 3D fold of a single polypeptide chain. Stabilised by hydrophobic core packing, disulfide bonds, salt bridges, and hydrogen bonds. This is what we see in PDB structures.

Quaternary (4°)

Assembly of multiple polypeptide chains (subunits) into a functional complex. Example: haemoglobin is a tetramer (α2β2). Many enzymes and receptors function as oligomers.

Key Structural Databases

PDB (rcsb.org)

~220,000 experimentally determined structures. Solved by X-ray crystallography (~87%), cryo-EM (~10%), or NMR (~3%). The primary source for known structures.

AlphaFold DB

~200 million predicted structures covering nearly all known proteins. Free access at alphafold.ebi.ac.uk. Quality indicated by pLDDT confidence score (blue = high, orange = low).

UniProt

Links sequence → structure → function. Cross-references PDB and AlphaFold entries. Shows domain architecture, active sites, binding sites, and post-translational modifications.

Reading pLDDT scores: AlphaFold provides a per-residue confidence score (pLDDT, 0–100). >90 = very high confidence (core domains). 70–90 = confident. 50–70 = low confidence (may be flexible). <50 = disordered/unstructured regions. Always check pLDDT before interpreting predicted structures.

What holds a fold together

  • The hydrophobic effect is the main driving force. Non-polar side chains (Leu, Ile, Val, Phe) pack into the core, away from water.
  • Hydrogen bonds between backbone N-H and C=O groups form the regular secondary structures.
  • Salt bridges between oppositely charged side chains (Lys or Arg with Asp or Glu) and disulfide bonds between cysteines add stability, the latter mostly in secreted proteins.

An α-helix has 3.6 residues per turn, and each backbone C=O bonds to the N-H four residues further on. A β-sheet is made of extended strands lying side by side, running in the same direction (parallel) or in opposite directions (antiparallel). The backbone can only take certain combinations of its two rotation angles, phi and psi; a Ramachandran plot shows them, and residues in forbidden regions are a standard sign of an error in a model.

Domains, motifs and families

Most proteins are built from domains: compact units of roughly 50 to 250 residues that fold on their own and are reused across many proteins. A kinase domain or an SH2 domain does the same job wherever it appears. Databases such as Pfam and InterPro find domains from sequence alone, and CATH and SCOP classify known structures by fold. Recognising a domain is often the fastest route to a first guess at function.

Reading a PDB entry critically

  • Resolution (in Å): lower is better. Under 2.0 Å side chains and waters are clear; above 3.5 Å only the overall fold is reliable.
  • R-free: how well the model predicts data that were held back during refinement. Values near 0.20 to 0.25 are typical for a good crystal structure.
  • B-factor: how much each atom moves or how uncertain its position is. High B-factors mark flexible loops.
  • What is missing: disordered loops and termini are often simply absent from the file. Check the sequence coverage before assuming a region has no structure.

AlphaFold2, developed by DeepMind, solved the protein structure prediction problem in 2020. It predicts 3D structures from amino acid sequence alone with near-experimental accuracy. ColabFold makes it easy to run AlphaFold predictions using Google Colab — no local GPU required.

How AlphaFold Works (Simplified)

(1) MSA generation — searches sequence databases to find homologous proteins (evolutionary information). (2) Template search — finds known structures of related proteins in PDB. (3) Evoformer — a deep neural network processes the MSA and pairwise features through 48 attention-based blocks. (4) Structure module — converts learned representations into 3D atomic coordinates. (5) Recycling — repeats the prediction 3 times, feeding output back as input, improving accuracy each time.

Limitations of AlphaFold: (1) Does not predict conformational changes — gives a single static structure. (2) Disordered regions (low pLDDT) should not be interpreted structurally. (3) Cannot predict effects of mutations reliably. (4) Ligand/cofactor binding sites may not be accurate without the ligand present. (5) AlphaFold2 struggles with protein-protein interfaces — use AlphaFold-Multimer or AlphaFold3 for complexes.

Why the alignment matters so much

Two residues that touch in the folded protein tend to change together during evolution: if one mutates, the other often mutates to compensate. A deep multiple sequence alignment reveals these co-evolving pairs, and they tell the network which residues are close in space. This is why a protein with thousands of known relatives is usually predicted well, and an orphan protein with few relatives is not.

PAE (in Å, per residue pair)Expected error in the position of one residue when the model is aligned on anotherLow PAE between two domains means their relative arrangement can be trusted

A common mistake: high pLDDT in two domains does not mean their relative position is correct. Two confident domains joined by a flexible linker can be placed anywhere. Always look at the PAE plot before drawing conclusions about how domains or chains sit together.

What a prediction cannot tell you

  • One static shape. Proteins move between conformations; a prediction gives one, usually the best-represented in the PDB.
  • Effect of point mutations. The model for a disease variant usually looks almost identical to the wild type. Predicted structures are not a stability calculator.
  • Ligands, ions and modifications. AlphaFold2 models the protein chain only. AlphaFold 3 and similar newer models add nucleic acids, small molecules and ions.
  • Disordered regions. Low-confidence ribbons are placeholders, not structure.

Molecular docking predicts how a small molecule (ligand/drug) binds to a protein target. It is a cornerstone of computer-aided drug design (CADD). The goal is to find the binding pose (orientation + conformation) and estimate binding affinity. Virtual screening uses docking to test millions of compounds computationally before synthesising the most promising ones.

Docking Tools

AutoDock Vina

The most widely used docking tool. Fast, accurate, free. Flexible ligand + rigid receptor. Easy to use via command line.

Gnina

CNN-based scoring function built on Vina. Better pose prediction than classical scoring. GPU-accelerated.

Diffusion model for docking. Does not require predefined binding site. State-of-the-art blind docking accuracy.

Docking limitations: Docking scoring functions are approximate — they estimate binding affinity with ~2 kcal/mol error. Use docking for ranking (which compounds bind better), not absolute binding energy prediction. Always validate top hits experimentally. For more accurate binding free energies, use molecular dynamics with free energy perturbation (FEP) or MM-PBSA methods.

Two separate problems: posing and scoring

Docking does two things. Posing asks where and in what orientation the ligand sits in the pocket. Scoring asks how strongly it binds. Current programs are good at the first and much weaker at the second: the correct pose is often among the top few, but the estimated energies correlate only loosely with measured affinities. Treat docking as a way to rank and filter candidates, not as a measurement.

For scale, binding energy and the dissociation constant are linked by ΔG = RT ln Kd. At 25 °C, every ten-fold improvement in Kd is worth about 1.4 kcal/mol, so a score difference of 0.5 kcal/mol between two compounds is well inside the error of the method.

Validate before you screen

  1. Re-dock the known ligand. Remove the ligand from the crystal structure and dock it back. A top pose within 2 Å RMSD of the crystal pose shows that your setup works.
  2. Test enrichment. Dock known binders mixed with decoys (similar properties, presumed inactive). The binders should rank near the top.
  3. Inspect poses by eye. Look for the hydrogen bonds and contacts that known ligands make. A good score with no sensible interactions is a warning.
