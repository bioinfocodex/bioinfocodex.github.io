It gets the published result

RNAflow is benchmarked on the airway study, a public dataset whose answer is well established. The commands the app generates are run unchanged and scored against the published counts and an independent DESeq2 analysis.

The software

RNAflow: a guided
RNA-seq analysis

Written for biologists who have sequencing data but not a bioinformatics background. Every step is a page that explains what it does and what good output looks like.

One click per step The app builds the commands for your samples and runs them. The commands stay visible, so the analysis can be read, checked and repeated.
The whole workflow Quality control, trimming, Salmon or HISAT2 with featureCounts, DESeq2 checked against edgeR and limma-voom, then GO, KEGG, Reactome and Hallmark enrichment.
Reproducible by default Every tool and R package is installed at a fixed version, and each run records the software behind its results.
Room to grow Generate SLURM, PBS or LSF job scripts, or export the project to nf-core/rnaseq when it outgrows a laptop.

How it is built

Made for people with data,
not for bioinformaticians

Your data stays on your machine
Analyses run locally and your reads are never uploaded. Only public lookups, such as a GEO accession, go to NCBI or Ensembl.
Everything is explained
Each command comes with a plain-English explanation, and each result is interpreted: volcano plots, heatmaps, alignment rates and DESeq2 tables.
Public data in one step
Paste a GEO or SRA accession to import every sample in a study, or start from a count table from your sequencing core.
15+ organisms ready
Human, mouse, Arabidopsis, rice, wheat, maize, soybean, tomato, zebrafish, Drosophila, C. elegans, yeast and more, with Ensembl genome and annotation addresses pre-loaded.
Open source
RNAflow is released under the MIT licence. There is no subscription and no paywalled feature, and the code and benchmarks are public.
Citable
Every release is archived on Zenodo with its own DOI, so the exact version you used can go in your methods. How to cite.
What comes next

RNAflow is the first tool. Pipelines for variant calling, ChIP-seq, single-cell RNA-seq and more are planned but not built yet.
